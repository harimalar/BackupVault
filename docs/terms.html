What the app backs up

BackupVault copies selected folders to a destination you control. It is not a bootable or full-system backup. Hidden files, symbolic links, and items matching configured exclusions are not copied. Files that cannot be read or change during copying can cause a run to fail. Optional verification compares file content but cannot guarantee future recoverability or protect against drive failure.

Choose the folders and destination carefully, keep sufficient free space, and periodically test a restore. Keep complete snapshot folders together and use Restore in BackupVault to recover Mac attributes stored in the snapshot metadata archives. Only copy or access files and servers you have permission to use.

Restore and automatic cleanup

Restoring files can replace matching files at the restore destination. Folder restores merge contents and retain unrelated existing files. Review the chosen restore location before starting.

Keep the last 10 backups is optional and off by default. Once enabled, it permanently deletes older recognized completed backups for this Mac in the same backup folder after a new backup succeeds. The newest successful backup is protected. Deleted snapshots cannot be recovered through BackupVault. Keep separate copies of any archives you want to retain indefinitely.

Scheduling, eject, and security

Automatic schedules require BackupVault to remain open and the destination to be available. Do not disconnect a drive during backup or restore.

Eject when finished is optional and off by default. It asks macOS to eject an external destination after a successful backup and cleanup. Wait for confirmation that the drive was safely ejected; if eject fails, leave it connected and use Finder. Network destinations remain connected.

Backups use normal files and folders. BackupVault does not add backup encryption. Secure your devices, network shares, and credentials appropriately.
